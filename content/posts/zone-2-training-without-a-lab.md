The problem with easy running is that it is not comfortable to do. Not physically, which is the point, but socially and psychologically. Running slowly feels like not training.

So most people don't. They run their easy days at a moderate effort that is too hard to recover from and too soft to drive adaptation, and then wonder why the hard sessions never improve.

## What zone 2 actually is

Zone 2 is the upper end of the intensity range where your body clears lactate as fast as it produces it. Below it, effort is trivially sustainable. Above it, lactate accumulates, and the clock starts.

The physiological target is mitochondrial density and fat oxidation — slow adaptations in slow-twitch fibres that need volume and time rather than intensity. Intensity develops other things. It does not develop this.

:::stat 80/20 | The intensity split observed across elite endurance sport: roughly four-fifths easy, one-fifth hard

## Three field tests, in order of reliability

Forget the watch for a moment. The zone has a feel, and the feel is more reliable than the number.

**The talk test.** You should be able to speak a full sentence, comfortably, without breaking it up to breathe. Not a few words. A sentence, with punctuation. If you find yourself editing what you were going to say to make it shorter, you are above zone 2.

**Nasal breathing.** Close your mouth. If you can maintain the effort breathing only through your nose, you are at or below the top of zone 2. The moment you need your mouth, you have crossed. This one is unusually good because it fails immediately and unmistakably.

**Heart rate.** Roughly 60 to 70 percent of true maximum. Useful, but third on the list for good reasons covered below.

:::callout Calibrate the number to the feel
Run twenty minutes at a pace where the talk test passes cleanly, then look at your average heart rate. That number is your zone 2 ceiling, and it is more trustworthy than anything a formula produced.
:::

## Why the watch is the least reliable of the three

Heart rate is a lagging, drifting, context-dependent signal.

| Factor | Effect on heart rate | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up lag | 1-2 min behind effort | Early readings understate the effort |
| Cardiac drift | +5-10 bpm over an hour | Same effort, rising number |
| Heat and humidity | +5-15 bpm | A hot day is a harder day at the same pace |
| Poor sleep | +3-8 bpm at rest | The whole curve shifts |
| Caffeine | +3-5 bpm | Not the same as more effort |
| Wrist optical sensor | Cadence lock, spikes | Use a chest strap for anything that matters |

Cadence lock is the one that misleads people most: an optical wrist sensor loses the pulse and reports your step rate instead, producing a stable, plausible, entirely fictional number. A chest strap does not do this.

## The 80/20 problem in practice

The rule is straightforward and the execution is not. Written out, a week might look like this:

1. **Monday** — rest or easy walk.
2. **Tuesday** — 45 min zone 2.
3. **Wednesday** — intervals, genuinely hard, 20 min of work inside a full session.
4. **Thursday** — 45 min zone 2.
5. **Friday** — rest.
6. **Saturday** — 30 min zone 2 with 6 x 20s strides at the end.
7. **Sunday** — 75-90 min zone 2.

Four and a half hours, of which roughly forty minutes are hard. That ratio is the whole design.

> Polarised training does not mean training less. It means the easy work is easy enough that the hard work can be hard.

The failure mode is drift. Week three, the easy runs creep up because you feel good. Week five, the hard session is slower because you never recovered from the easy ones. The distinction has collapsed and the plan is now one long moderate effort.

- [x] Easy days pass the talk test for the entire run, including the last ten minutes
- [x] Hard days are hard enough that you are glad when they end
- [ ] Weekly hard minutes stay roughly constant while easy volume grows
- [ ] You are willing to walk the hills to keep an easy day easy

That last one is the tell. Walking a hill to protect the zone is discipline, not weakness, and most people cannot bring themselves to do it.

## What progress looks like

Not a faster easy pace this week. The signal is that in two or three months, the same conversational effort corresponds to a pace that used to require concentration. Same heart rate, more speed.

That is aerobic base arriving. It is slow, it is unglamorous, and it is the only foundation the sharper work has to stand on.
